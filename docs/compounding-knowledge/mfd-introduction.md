# Mapped Frontier Development: map the unknown, build with intent, prove the outcome
DRAFT — educational text, not canonical policy and not approved for public release.
MFD is a method for turning ambiguous product ideas into mapped goals, executable slices and verifiable outcomes. Start with what the product promises and who benefits; identify what is known, unknown and undecided. Describe the intended behavior and evidence before implementation. Make dependencies and decisions explicit, then execute small slices and update the map as evidence arrives.
## Product lifecycle
1. Describe audience, purpose, outcome and scope.
2. Map features, dependencies, decisions, visuals and deliverables.
3. Define goals, milestones and layered verification; explain nonapplicable proof.
4. Implement bounded slices, review and test.
5. Demonstrate behavior and record evidence; distinguish source readiness from deployed acceptance.
6. Package, release, operate, learn and feed discoveries into the next map.
## Example
A fictional team-roster application needs a searchable fighter catalog, five-slot builder, identity and saved teams. Specify the screen, art assets, interaction rules, APIs, unit and integration tests, mobile journey, deployment and demo before calling it shipped.
